  - id: arriving
    icon: "01"
    title: Arriving in the US
    items:
      - title: Airport to Campus
        desc: Before you land, save your campus address, dorm check-in address, and international office phone number. At the airport, connect to Wi-Fi, open Uber or Lyft, enter the exact dorm/building address, and confirm the license plate before getting in.
      - title: Your First 48 Hours
        desc: Check into housing, tell family you arrived, buy basic groceries, then visit your international student office, complete arrival check-in, get your student ID, activate your .edu email, and confirm your SEVIS/I-20 record is active.
      - title: Phone, SIM, and Internet
        desc: A U.S. phone number makes banking, rideshare, apartment applications, and two-factor login easier. Start with prepaid if you do not have a Social Security number yet.
      - title: Documents to Keep Handy
        desc: Keep digital and paper copies of your passport, visa, I-20, admission letter, housing address, emergency contacts, immunization record, and insurance card.
  - id: banking
    icon: "02"
    title: Banking & Money
    items:
      - title: Opening a U.S. Bank Account
        desc: Most banks ask for passport, visa, I-20, student ID or admission letter, U.S. address, phone number, and sometimes proof of enrollment. If a large bank is difficult, try a local credit union near campus.
      - title: Building Credit Safely
        desc: Debit cards do not build credit. Ask about a student or secured credit card, use it for small purchases, and pay the full balance every month.
      - title: Budgeting Your First Semester
        desc: Plan for bedding, winter clothes, utensils, textbooks, transportation, security deposit, and health fees. Track rent, groceries, phone, subscriptions, and rideshares.
      - title: Scholarships and Emergency Funds
        desc: Search your department, graduate college, international office, and Dean of Students pages. Many universities have emergency grants for rent, food, medical bills, or urgent travel.
  - id: housing
    icon: "03"
    title: Housing
    items:
      - title: On-Campus vs Off-Campus
        desc: On-campus housing is easier for first-semester students. Off-campus can be cheaper, but check commute time, safety, lease length, utilities, laundry, and whether the apartment is furnished.
      - title: Finding Roommates
        desc: Use official university boards first, then chapter WhatsApp groups, Pakistani student groups, and trusted alumni referrals. Ask about guests, rent split, cleaning, parking, and lease responsibility.
      - title: Before You Sign a Lease
        desc: Read rent amount, deposit, move-in fee, utilities, sublease rules, early termination fee, and maintenance process. Take photos/video on move-in day.
      - title: Furniture and Setup
        desc: Start with Facebook Marketplace, university free-and-sale groups, Goodwill, IKEA basics, and graduating-student sales. Avoid buying too much before seeing the room.
  - id: career
    icon: "04"
    title: Career & Jobs
    items:
      - title: OPT, CPT, and Your DSO
        desc: Your Designated School Official is the first stop for work authorization questions. Do not begin paid work until authorization is approved and dates match the job.
      - title: U.S. Resume Format
        desc: Use one page if early-career. No photo, date of birth, marital status, religion, CNIC, or full address. Use action verbs, numbers, and impact.
      - title: Networking Without Feeling Awkward
        desc: Ask for 15 minutes, not a job. Message alumni with one clear reason and one specific question. Follow up once, politely, after a week.
      - title: Internships and Sponsorship
        desc: Start early. Use your career center, Handshake, LinkedIn alumni search, and UPSA chapter networks to identify realistic sponsor-friendly employers.
  - id: culture
    icon: "05"
    title: Culture & Community
    items:
      - title: Finding Community Fast
        desc: Join your PSA/MSA/UPSA chapter, one academic club, and one hobby club. Community is often built by showing up repeatedly.
      - title: Halal Food and Groceries
        desc: Search halal restaurants, South Asian grocery stores, and mosque community boards near campus. Ask older students where they buy meat, spices, rice, and frozen meals.
      - title: Eid, Ramadan, and Classes
        desc: Email professors early for Eid or Ramadan accommodations. Many universities have religious accommodation policies.
      - title: Staying Connected Without Burning Out
        desc: Set predictable call windows with family and build a local support circle. Homesickness is normal; isolation makes it worse.
  - id: health
    icon: "06"
    title: Health & Insurance
    items:
      - title: Understanding Student Insurance
        desc: Learn your insurance card, deductible, copay, urgent care, emergency room, and in-network provider rules. The wrong clinic can cost much more.
      - title: Campus Health Center
        desc: Start with campus health for routine illness, vaccines, prescriptions, and referrals. Save the after-hours nurse line if your university has one.
      - title: Mental Health Support
        desc: Every university has a counseling center or mental health services office. You can ask for help with anxiety, homesickness, grief, academic pressure, or panic.
      - title: Prescriptions from Pakistan
        desc: Bring a list of medicines with generic names, dosage, and doctor notes. U.S. pharmacies may not recognize Pakistani brand names.
